I am in class

Change the voice in three steps

Every passive sentence is built from an active one. Tap through the steps with Ravi and the ball.

Pronouns change too

When a pronoun is the doer, use its “by” form in the passive.

I → by me
We → by us
He → by him
She → by her
They → by them
You → by you
It → by it

Practice time

When do we use the passive?

The doer is unknown

My bike was stolen.

The receiver matters more

The Charminar was built in 1591.

Science, notices and rules

Water is boiled at 100°C. Silence is requested.

Only sentences with a receiver can turn passive

“The bell rings.” has no receiver, so it stays active. Ask yourself: who or what receives the action? If nothing does, there is nothing to bring to the front.

For the teacher

Objectives and lesson flow

Learners will be able to

  • tell active and passive sentences apart by finding the doer, action and receiver;
  • change sentences from active to passive in the tenses listed for their class;
  • choose the voice that suits the purpose of a sentence.

Time: two periods of about 40 minutes. Materials: a projector or phone, pink, yellow and green chalk (doer, action, receiver).

  1. Hook (5 min). Project the Voices switch. Ask: who comes first now?
  2. Teach (10 min). Walk through the three steps. Colour each word on the board with the same chalk colours.
  3. Tense helper (10 min). Pick your class on the bar. Students copy the formulas and say each example aloud.
  4. Spot the voice (8 min). Teams answer on the count of three. Discuss every wrong answer.
  5. Build the sentence (7 min). Pairs decide the order before anyone taps.
  6. Exit ticket. Each student turns three active sentences into passive on a slip of paper.
Common mistakes to watch for
  • Using the verb itself (“was kick”) instead of be + V3 (“was kicked”).
  • Forgetting to change the pronoun (“by he” instead of “by him”).
  • Keeping the active tense: “is eating” must become “is being eaten”, not “is eaten”.
  • Trying to make a passive from a sentence with no receiver.
