What is a clause?

A clause is a group of words with a subject and a verb. Without both, it is only a phrase.

subject + verb = clause
Phrase: no subject and verb pair

in the garden

a bunch of red roses

Clause: has a subject and a verb

the dog barked

because it was late

Main clause: makes complete sense alone

The dog barked.

Subordinate clause: leaves us waiting

Because it was late…

For the teacher

Objectives and lesson flow

Learners will be able to

  • tell a clause from a phrase, and a main clause from a subordinate clause (Class 6);
  • use coordinate and adverb clauses, and spot adjective clauses (Class 7);
  • identify noun, adjective and adverb clauses, and name simple, compound and complex sentences (Class 8).

Time: two periods of about 40 minutes. Materials: a projector or phone, teal, yellow and pink chalk (main clause, joiner, subordinate clause).

  1. Hook (5 min). Say “because it was raining” and stop. Ask: what happened? Students feel the missing half.
  2. Tap test (8 min). Project the hero. Students tap each clause and decide: does it stand alone?
  3. Clause helper (12 min). Pick your class on the bar. Read each card, then colour the clauses on the board.
  4. Clause detective (5 min). Take one textbook sentence and answer the detective’s questions together.
  5. Practice (8 min). Teams play Test it, Join it and Type it on the count of three.
  6. Exit ticket. Each student writes three sentences, each with a main clause and a subordinate clause, and circles the joiner.
Common mistakes to watch for
  • Calling a phrase a clause. Check for a subject and a verb together.
  • Ending a sentence with only the subordinate clause (“Because it was raining.”). This is a fragment.
  • Thinking the subordinate clause is less important. It only means the clause cannot stand alone.
  • Forgetting the comma when the subordinate clause comes first.
  • Mixing up “and, but, or” (they join equal clauses) with “because, when, if” (they start a subordinate clause).
  • Missing noun clauses. Ask: can I replace the clause with “it” or “something”?
